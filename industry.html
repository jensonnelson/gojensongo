Apparel for the people already wearing the shoe

A graphics and allover print franchise built for hospitality floor and kitchen staff. One print engine, three scales, and a season two that costs almost nothing to option.

Concept visualization / Not affiliated with any brand
01

The insight

While I've been looking for the right next design role, I've been serving and bartending at a brewery. Unexpectedly, it's given me a pretty great piece of first-hand consumer research. We can wear whatever we want, and almost everyone on the floor wears the same running shoe. Nobody decided that. There's no uniform, no brand partnership, no trend memo. It happened one recommendation at a time.

When you're on your feet for eight hours, carrying trays, moving constantly, and literally running up and down three flights of stairs, the shoes make their case pretty quickly. The brand didn't have to win us over. The work did that. What struck me as a designer is that the shoe has already won the room, but we want more to wear with it.

That's where this capsule started.

First-hand, not a trend board
01

The consumer is already here

This room did not have to be earned. Restaurants, bars, hotels, catering. Same shift shape as healthcare, which the brand already serves with slip resistant product. The apparel side has simply never been pointed at it.

02

It is a refresh problem, not a new brand

The whole franchise runs on one print system that recolors and rescales every season. That is cheap newness on a fast calendar, which is what an apparel refresh engine needs.

03

It is built for SMU

Restaurant groups buy staff apparel in volume and want their own name on it. The print below is drawn so a group's own language drops into the artwork. Same engine, unlimited versions.

Hospitality is hard on color. Heat, bleach, grease, sweat, and forty washes a season. Solid mid-tones show everything; black fades, white yellows, and the staff keeps wearing it anyway. This palette is designed around that reality. Bleach and wear become part of the range, while darker grounds, cold neutrals, and broken color help disguise what a long shift leaves behind.

03

Print family

Four prints, three colorways each, every one drawn from something that is already in the building. Type, paperwork, line, stain. Guest Check, Behind and Table Rings tile seamlessly; Ticket Rail is a continuous feed that wraps, so it is placed rather than repeated.
P01

Ticket Rail

The calls, set as continuous type and left to wrap. Phrases break across line ends the way they come off the rail, so the block reads as texture from across the room and as language from arm's length. Heard, full hands in, in the weeds, I got you. It runs on any of the six grounds without being redrawn, which makes it the cheapest piece in the range to option.

Scale
Micro to mid, depending on ground
Technique
One-color type print
Application
Crewneck, tee, liner, pocket bag
P02

Guest Check

The guest check, rebuilt as a grid. Tables, covers, mods, the tally down the side, the thank you at the bottom. From a distance it reads as a woven check. Up close it is paperwork, which is most of the job. This is the lightest print in the family and the only one that wants a light ground.

Scale
Mid / 1:2
Technique
Two-color line print, holds detail down to a sock
Application
Camp shirt, tee, tote, liner
P03

Behind

Hospitality has its own traffic system. Behind, corner, hands, clear. Everyone learns it in a week and nobody ever writes it down. This draws it as wayfinding: the routes through the room and the calls you make so nobody wears a tray. It is the only print here built on line instead of type or stain, which is what keeps the family from repeating itself.

Scale
Mid / 1:2
Technique
Three-color line print
Application
Cap crown, short, bandana, shell lining
P04

Table Rings

Beer rings. What glasses and steins leave on the bar, on the towel, on the table you wiped twice. Overlapped until it stops reading as a stain and starts reading as camo, which is also the function, because it hides what hospitality does to a garment. Pattern from across the room, evidence up close.

Scale
Large / 1:1 engineered
Technique
Placed sublimation, cut so no two pieces crop the same
Application
Service short, side towel, tote, outer layer
04

Graphic hits

INDUSTRY

The signature wordmark. Tight, balanced lettering that stays clear at small sizes, woven or embroidered.

86

Kitchen shorthand for out of stock. Works as a chest hit, a hem tag, or a sock cuff.

ALL DAY

The running total for an order. Set as a repeat band along the short hem.

HEARD

I got you. Placed front and center on a hat or tee, turning the acknowledgment into a public statement.

Season two

Carry
Ticket Rail

Franchise anchor. Recolor only, no redraw. This is the piece the consumer comes back for, so it does not move.

Refresh
Guest Check

Recolor and rescale. The grid holds up at any size, so a new colorway reads new on the floor without new artwork.

Replace
Table Rings

Retire and rebuild the large organic each season against whatever the season turns out to be about. This is the newness slot.

The SMU mechanic

Ticket Rail is built from a text file. Swap the text and the print becomes a specific restaurant group's own shorthand, their own 86 list, their own table numbers, in their own colorway. The artwork engine never changes and the feed keeps running. One file, one approval path, and as many custom programs as sales can close.

This is the part I would push hardest. It turns a capsule into a repeatable revenue mechanic without adding a single creation calendar.
