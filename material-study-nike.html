One brand runs two opposite material playbooks. Reading which one a product needs is the whole job. Here are the Air Force 1 and NikeCourt, side by side.

Air Force 1 · 1982

The problem was love. People wanted the leather, so you re-engineer it instead of replacing it.

1982 — Origin
Full-grain leather. Kilgore's white high-top. The material was the identity.
Mid-2000s — Proof
Primeknit, patent, Hyperfuse, Gore-Tex. The silhouette proves it can carry any material.
Now — Flyleather
50%+ recycled leather fiber from tannery scrap, water-fused with synthetics. Keeps the look, feel, and smell of premium hide.

NikeCourt Women's · 1991→

The problem was cotton. The material failed the athlete, so you kill it and keep only the benefit.

Pre-1991 — Origin
Cotton. Soaks up sweat, turns heavy, fights the body's cooling.
1991 — Dri-FIT
Microfiber polyester wicks sweat to the surface to evaporate. Enters tennis by way of Olympic kit.
Now — Recycled poly + ADV
Yarn spun from plastic bottles. Dri-FIT ADV maps fabric zones to where players sweat most.

The through-line

Solve performance first. Re-engineer at lower impact. Never ask the wearer to sacrifice.

Different problems, same philosophy. The sustainable version was never pitched as accept this because it's greener. It was pitched as this is simply better — lighter, more durable, tuned to the body.
